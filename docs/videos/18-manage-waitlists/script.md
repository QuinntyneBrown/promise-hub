# 18 · Manage waitlists

When a camp fills up, families go on the waitlist with a number. Then someone drops out, or the camp adds staff, and a place opens. This video shows how Marisol, the program director, moves the next child in, and what the family and the rest of the waitlist see.

## What the waitlist promises

The waitlist is kept in order, one, two, three, with no gaps. While places are free, each waitlisted child has a Move in button. Moving a child in puts them on the roster, texts the guardian, and moves everyone behind them up one place. When the program is full, the button is off and the page says why.

## Marisol moves Kevin in

Here is the Winter Camp roster on Marisol's laptop. On the Waitlist tab, Kevin is number one and Emily number two. She taps Move in beside Kevin, adds a note, and confirms. Linh is texted, Kevin is on the roster, and Emily is now number one.

## Full programs and other roles

If Winter Camp were full, Move in would be switched off with a short reason, and the server would refuse anyway with the count. Site leads can't move children in, and another organization's waitlist is never found.

## How it is tested

The backend tests put two children on the waitlist, move the first one in, and check the enrolled count, the guardian's text with the note, and the second child moving up to number one. The browser tests do the same on phone and desktop, check the disabled button when the program is full, check the module switched off, and run an accessibility check on the waitlist.

## Recap

Things to remember. The waitlist stays in order with no gaps. Move in enrolls the child and texts the guardian. Everyone behind moves up. Next, the youth worker's day.
