# 20 · Report an incident as a youth worker

Youth workers are often the first adult on the scene. They need to report what happened as easily as a site lead does, without seeing reports that aren't theirs. This video shows Aisha reporting a reaction at snack time, and what she can and cannot read afterwards.

## What a youth worker can do

A youth worker uses the same form as everyone else, with the same facts in the same order, and the report goes to the program director the same way. Afterwards she can open her own reports. The log of everyone's reports is for supervisors only, and a link to someone else's report answers as if it didn't exist.

## Aisha reports Jordan's reaction

Here is Aisha's phone. She chooses illness or allergic reaction and Jordan, the time and the room, writes that his lips swelled after snack, that she gave his antihistamine and called Dana, and marks it moderate. She submits, and her report opens: needs review.

## What she can read

In the log, Aisha sees the supervisors-only message and her own reports underneath. When she follows a link to Jaylen's report, the page says it can't be found, and the server answers not found rather than forbidden, so she can't tell it exists.

## How it is tested

A backend test has a youth worker and a volunteer coordinator each report an incident, list only their own, and get not found for a site lead's report. The browser tests follow Aisha on phone and desktop with an accessibility check on the form and the log.

## Recap

Things to remember. One form for everyone. Your own reports, never anyone else's. Not found rather than forbidden. Next, reviewing incidents.
