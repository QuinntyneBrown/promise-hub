# 30 · Start an invited shift

A new youth worker's first shift starts with an email. This video shows Sam going from Tom's invitation to tonight's check-in in under a minute, on a phone, with nothing to install.

## What starting a shift promises

The invitation link shows which organization Sam is joining, the role, who invited him, and the email he'll sign in with. He chooses a name and a password of at least twelve characters. The account belongs to that organization only, and the link works once. Then he gives a mobile number for alerts, which guardians never see, and the last step shows the sessions he's working today with one tap to open check-in. An old or withdrawn link says so and names who to ask.

## Sam accepts on his phone

Here is Sam's phone. He opens the link from Tom's email. It says he's joining Harbourview Youth Collective as a youth worker. He enters his name and a passphrase and accepts. Next he types his mobile with the area code and continues. The last screen says he's ready and shows tutoring night, with the button to open check-in.

## Mistakes and old links

A short password or a phone number without its area code is named right under the field, and nothing moves on until it's fixed. If the link has expired, Sam is told to ask Tom for a new one.

## How it is tested

Backend tests cover an open invitation's details, expired and unknown links, accepting into the right organization only with a single use, the audit entry, missing names and short passwords, and the phone rule. The browser tests follow Sam from the link to the ready screen on phone and desktop, including the mistakes and an expired link, with accessibility checks.

## Recap

Things to remember. One link, one organization, used once. A mobile for alerts that guardians never see. The first screen after is tonight's work. Next, coordinating staff coverage.
