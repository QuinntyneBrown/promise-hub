# 26 · Manage approved pickups

At the end of every session, a child goes home with an adult. Staff need to know which adults the family has approved, and families need to change that list without a phone call. This video shows how Linh adds her sister from her phone, and how Jaylen manages the same list from a child's Quick Card.

## What approved pickups promise

Guardians who may pick up are always on the list. Besides them, the family or staff can approve other adults, by name and relationship, for one child or all the family's children. A new adult appears on the Quick Card and in sign-out at once. Removing someone keeps them in the history, so past sign-outs still make sense, and when staff remove someone the primary guardian is texted. Every change is recorded.

## Linh adds her sister

Here is Linh's family page, after the code check. Under Kevin, it lists who may pick him up besides her. She taps Add someone for Kevin, types her sister Mai's name and that she's his aunt, and adds her. Mai is on the list straight away, and the staff will see her too. If Mai ever stops collecting Kevin, Linh removes her the same way, from the same page, and no one needs to call the office.

## Staff on the Quick Card

On Mateo's Quick Card, Jaylen can add an approved adult for Mateo alone or for all the family's children, and remove one with a confirmation that tells him the guardian will be texted. Youth workers see the list but can't change it.

## How it is tested

Backend tests cover adding with missing details, adding for siblings, the Quick Card and sign-out lists, removal keeping history with the text and the audit entry, youth workers refused, a guardian adding and removing after the code check, another household's child refused, another organization and the module switched off. The browser tests follow Jaylen on the Quick Card and Linh on her family page, with accessibility checks.

## Recap

Things to remember. Guardians plus approved adults, on one list. Families and staff can change it. Nothing is ever lost from the history. Next, sending messages to families.
