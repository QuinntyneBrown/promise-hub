# 62 · Submit assignments

An assignment is how a youth shows what she learned, and the main proof of learning her mentor grades. This video shows how a youth submits one in PromiseHub: she reads the brief, sends a reflection and a photo with a promise that it is her own work, follows it through review, and revises and resubmits when her mentor asks. Her unsent work is protected along the way.

## The brief

Sofía Rivera is in After-School Tutoring at Harbourview, and she has done Pick Up the Towel. Its assignment, Serve someone this week, now shows on the module page as up next, with its points and due date. Opening it shows the brief: the task, when it is due and how many days are left, what she can send, the rubric with every criterion and its points, and who reviews it, her mentor Daniel Osei. An assignment attached to a lesson stays locked until that lesson is done, and the server answers locked and names what to finish first.

## Sending her work

Start submission opens the form. It shows only what this assignment takes: here a reflection and a photo. She writes a few sentences, chooses a photo, and ticks that this is her own work and something she really did. Submit opens a dialog that says she cannot change it while it is being reviewed. On confirming, the work and the photo travel together in one request. The server checks everything again, stores the photo privately under her organization, records the submission as Submitted, audits it without the text, and opens its status page.

## What the server checks

A photo must be a JPG or a PNG, and a file a PDF, a JPG or a PNG, of up to ten megabytes, and its name, its type and its first bytes must agree. Anything else is refused and nothing is stored. The reflection is limited to five thousand characters, and a link must be a full web address. Without the promise, or without any work, the page shows the errors beside the fields and in a summary. A second submission is refused. After the due date and the grace period she can still submit when late work is allowed, and it is marked late for her mentor; otherwise submissions are closed.

## Following it

Her submission page shows what she sent and what happened, in order, with who did it and when. When Daniel starts reviewing, it says In review. If he asks for a revision, his note shows at the top, and the assignment and the module say Needs revision. When he accepts it, she sees his comment and the points awarded, twenty five of twenty five. The review itself arrives with grading, in the next video.

## Revising

Revise your submission opens the same form with Daniel's note above her last reflection. Her photo is kept unless she removes or replaces it. She adds what she noticed, confirms it is her own work again, and resubmits. It goes back to Submitted, the history gains a Resubmitted step, and the change is audited. Only work that was sent back can be resubmitted; anything else is refused.

## Protecting unsent work

If she leaves the form with changes she has not sent, a dialog asks first: keep editing, save a draft and leave, or leave without saving. Closing or reloading the tab asks too. A draft keeps her reflection and link on this device, under her own membership, so nobody else signing in there sees it. When she comes back, the assignment offers to continue her draft, and the form restores it. A photo is never kept in a draft, and sending the work clears it.

## On a phone

On a phone the brief, the form and her submission stack in one column, with no sideways scrolling. Choose photo, Save draft and Submit stay large enough to tap with a thumb.

## The other states

The form shows every error at once. A file that does not fit is not attached, and the field says what fits. A late or closed assignment says so. A locked assignment names the lesson to finish first. Another organization's assignment is not found, a failed load offers Try again, and with Learning off these pages go to Today while every endpoint answers not found.

## How it is tested

The API tests cover the brief, the locks and the due rules, submitting with a file, every refusal, late and closed work, the status through review, revising with the file kept, replaced or removed, another organization, other roles and Learning off. The browser tests submit, revise and save drafts on a phone and a laptop against the fake API, with accessibility checks.

## Recap

Things to remember. The work and its file travel together and are checked on the server. Once sent it is read only, until her mentor asks for a revision. Unsent work is protected with a draft on her device. Next, mentors grade and assess.
